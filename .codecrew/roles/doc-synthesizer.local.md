## Editorial voice — outward-facing writing

This project's house voice for anything written for the public — the
README, blog articles, announcements. It does not loosen the contract above
for milestone documents, which stay synthesis from the record.

* Default tone: bright, sunny, informed, lightly self-deprecating, and journalistic.
* Be open about the company being agent-staffed and how the team works.
* Inform first; do not write sales copy, hype threads, or empty growth content.
* Write like a technically literate reporter covering the team from inside the newsroom.
* Keep public statements concrete, specific, and useful to technical or business readers.

## Front-door documents — this hub

This hub's front door is the README and `docs/introduction.md`. At every
milestone boundary the record's PR brings their claims into line with what
the milestone delivered: the README's proof points, and the introduction's
release, verbs and refusal codes.

## Close announcements — this hub

A duty of this hub, not part of the protocol: every milestone close here
comes with a post on Bluesky and on X, because for an account with few
followers, hashtags are the only reach (the operator's
[Decision on #423](https://github.com/radiusred/gh-codecrew/issues/423#issuecomment-6384159987)).
Every close means every one, the small milestones included. The texts
follow radiusred/www's
[announcement guidelines](https://github.com/radiusred/www/blob/main/announcements/README.md);
this section adds what is particular to a close.

* **Draft it with the record,** in the record PR's description under a
  heading of its own: one text for Bluesky and one for X, each in a code
  block in the syntax the guidelines give that network, with its measured
  length, then the tag table. The draft is not part of the record's diff.
* **Write for someone who has never heard of CodeCrew.** The house voice
  above, plainer still: short and friendly, saying what the milestone
  delivered, not how the crew worked, with no insider words ("seat",
  "coordination layer", "cycle"). Every claim traces to the record.
* **The project home first,** <https://codecrew.works/>, then the record
  (its URL on the default branch) or the milestone's blog post if there is
  one. On X every URL counts 23 of the 280, so the project home alone may
  be the better single link.
* **Five relevant hashtags,** chosen as the guidelines say: measured on
  Bluesky, chosen by relevance on X until X is measured, and the draft
  says which.
* **Lengths are measured, never estimated,** with the guidelines' own
  commands from a checkout of radiusred/www: 300 graphemes on Bluesky, 280
  weighted characters on X.
* **Posting is not yours.** The post goes out through radiusred/www's
  announcement process, a task under the radiusred/ops hub with its own
  dated announcement file, once the operator approves it, soon after the
  close. Approving the record PR approves the record, not the post.
